Power: the chapter written by smoke

Nothing in this section came from a tutorial. Each lesson cost us an afternoon, a component, or a small heart attack.

LESSON 01 · THE 67 W TRAP

A laptop charger is not a power supply

We plugged a 67 W USB-PD brick into the board and got… nothing. ~0 mA. USB-PD negotiates voltage through a handshake chip the ESP32 doesn't have, so the charger politely offered 5 V at a trickle. The fix was gloriously dumb: a 5 V / 2 A phone adapter. Dumb power is honest power.

LESSON 02 · THE SAG ABSORBER

1000 µF of forgiveness

A pump starting and a WiFi burst both demand current now. The rail dipped, the ESP32 browned out, the boot looped. A 1000 µF electrolytic capacitor strapped across 5 V/GND eats those spikes whole.

LESSON 03 · THE INDUCTIVE KICK

1N4007: the bodyguard diode

Switch a motor off and its coil fires a voltage spike backwards down the wire — straight at the GPIO. A 1N4007 flyback diode across the pump gives that spike a safe loop to die in.

LESSON 04 · THE ISOLATION WARD

The pump lives on its own circuit

The relay's COM/NO contacts put the pump on a separate 5 V source. Logic side and motor side share a ground reference and nothing else — pump noise never flows through the brain.

THE MYSTERY, SOLVED

"PSRAM not found" was never about RAM

The CAM refused to boot, screaming about missing PSRAM. The RAM was fine — the power wasn't. Weak 5 V = brownout during camera init. The 5 V / 2 A adapter fixed a "memory error" without touching a single line of code. Hardware bugs love to dress up as software bugs.

Our power checklist (tape it to your table)

Dumb 5 V / 2 A adapter · 1000 µF across the rails · diode across the pump · pump on relay-isolated supply · rails bridged + to +, − to −. Five lines, zero reboots.
