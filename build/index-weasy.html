02 · The whyPROBLEM

Plants don't die of thirst.
They die of silence.

Nobody kills their plant on purpose. They kill it with a busy week — because a plant has no voice, no notification, no way to say "I'm dry," "you're drowning me," or "the tank is empty."

Forgotten

Urban families travel, study late, work shifts. Watering slips a day, then a week. The soil says nothing — it just quietly hits zero.

Drowned

The opposite sin: daily "just in case" watering. Roots rot in soggy soil while the plant looks, from above, perfectly loved.

Blind

No one knows the soil moisture right now, whether the tank is empty, or that rain is coming tonight. Plants die of missing information.

₹8,000+Commercial smart-garden kits

And for that money? No camera. No AI doctor. A sealed black box a student can't open, understand, or repair. We priced the parts at our local market and did the maths: the gap between "product" and "project" wasn't money — it was information.

05 · HardwarePOWER · HARD-WON

Power: the chapter written by smoke

Nothing in this section came from a tutorial. Each lesson cost us an afternoon, a component, or a small heart attack.

LESSON 01 · THE 67 W TRAP

A laptop charger is not a power supply

We plugged a 67 W USB-PD brick into the board and got… nothing. ~0 mA. USB-PD negotiates voltage through a handshake chip the ESP32 doesn't have, so the charger politely offered 5 V at a trickle. The fix was gloriously dumb: a 5 V / 2 A phone adapter. Dumb power is honest power.

LESSON 02 · THE SAG ABSORBER

1000 µF of forgiveness

A pump starting and a WiFi burst both demand current now. The rail dipped, the ESP32 browned out, the boot looped. A 1000 µF electrolytic capacitor strapped across 5 V/GND eats those spikes whole.

LESSON 03 · THE INDUCTIVE KICK

1N4007: the bodyguard diode

Switch a motor off and its coil fires a voltage spike backwards down the wire — straight at the GPIO. A 1N4007 flyback diode across the pump gives that spike a safe loop to die in.

LESSON 04 · THE ISOLATION WARD

The pump lives on its own circuit

The relay's COM/NO contacts put the pump on a separate 5 V source. Logic side and motor side share a ground reference and nothing else — pump noise never flows through the brain.

THE MYSTERY, SOLVED

"PSRAM not found" was never about RAM

The CAM refused to boot, screaming about missing PSRAM. The RAM was fine — the power wasn't. Weak 5 V = brownout during camera init. The 5 V / 2 A adapter fixed a "memory error" without touching a single line of code. Hardware bugs love to dress up as software bugs.

Our power checklist (tape it to your table)

Dumb 5 V / 2 A adapter · 1000 µF across the rails · diode across the pump · pump on relay-isolated supply · rails bridged + to +, − to −. Five lines, zero reboots.

06 · FirmwareDECISION LOGIC

AUTO mode, drawn to scale

The whole watering brain in one diagram. Three locks must open before a single drop moves — soil says dry, tank says safe, sky says dry.

Three locks, one pump

  • Dry-soil consent — watering starts only below the threshold you set (35% default).
  • Tank lock — an empty tank means a dead pump if it ran dry. Level under 15%? Refused, even in manual.
  • Rain veto — the app watches Delhi's sky and can bar watering when rain is due.

The light has its own brain

LDR says "dark" (past the 35 line)? The UV grow LED switches on — with ±2% hysteresis so a passing cloud at dusk doesn't start a strobe party.

TESTED, NOT THEORISED

Pump AUTO ran a full 120-second soak with zero glitches, then switched off exactly as moisture crossed the threshold. Tank lock and rain veto were forced to fire on camera.

07 · The crime sceneBUG STORY

The bug that nearly sank us

AUTO mode was supposed to water until the soil hit 35%. Instead, the pump clicked on and off every ~10 seconds, like it had hiccups. This is the honest account of why.

THE SYMPTOM

tap. tap. tap.

In AUTO, the pump ran in ~10-second bursts instead of one continuous drink. The relay sounded like a telegraph key. Moisture climbed in zig-zags, not a slope.

THE FALSE LEADS

We blamed hardware. Of course we did.

Loose relay? Reseated. Power sag? Added the capacitor — it helped the rails, not the rhythm. Threshold noise? The filters were already clean. The stutter survived everything.

THE CLUE

The whole chip was rebooting, not the relay

We timestamped the serial log and saw it: a watchdog reboot every ~8–10 seconds, right in the pump's rhythm. The firmware wasn't deciding to stop — it was dying mid-decision and waking up in a different state.

THE ROOT CAUSE

17 blocking HTTPS calls per second

One Firebase call per sensor value, per control key, blocking, every loop. The network stalled, the loop starved, the 8-second watchdog fired, the chip rebooted, AUTO re-decided from scratch. On. Off. On. Off. tap. tap. tap.

08 · The fixBEFORE / AFTER

JSON bundling: the ~85% latency diet

Stop making one call per value. Pack everything into two envelopes — one up, one down. The fix took an evening; the lesson is permanent.

BEFORE · 17 CALLS / SECOND
17
  • One blocking HTTPS call per metric & per key
  • Network stalls starve the loop
  • Watchdog reboots all day
  • Pump: ~10-second stutter
AFTER · 2 CALLS / SECOND
  • 1 PUT — all 10 metrics to /sensors
  • 1 GET — all 9 keys from /controls
  • Latency down ~85%, zero watchdog reboots
  • Pump: ON until threshold, then off. Once.
// every second, exactly two envelopes:
PUT /sensors.json  ← { moisture, temperature, humidity, light, tank_level,
            lux, watchdog_status, voltage_sag, successful_uploads, failed_uploads }
GET /controls.json → { manual_mode, pump_state, light_manual_mode, grow_light_state,
            capture_photo, moisture_threshold, tank_threshold, light_threshold, weather_override }
~85%latency cut
0watchdog reboots
120 spump soak · clean
10+ mintorture test · 0 crashes
What it taught us

Count your network calls like coins. A microcontroller doesn't grief you for bad logic — it griefs you for chattering. Two fat envelopes beat seventeen thin letters.
